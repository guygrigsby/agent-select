The delta introduced one new blocker: `record_terms_observation` was added to contracts.md but delivery-storage.md — the delivery authority for operator ports and event schemas — was not amended, and its `ObservationRecorded` emission rule directly forbids the emission contracts assigns to the new port.

## Findings

**1. [BLOCKER] `record_terms_observation` is absent from the delivery-storage operator surface, and its `ObservationRecorded` emission is forbidden by the delivery event rule and unsatisfiable against the payload schema.**

Three concrete defects, one root cause (the delivery authority was never amended for the new port):

- delivery-storage.md owns the surface: "Owns the operator, collector worker, coordinator, publisher and host reporter mutation ports, the event payload schemas … This document supersedes the service, event and data surface in [contracts](contracts.md) for the implemented slice." Its Operator section lists exactly `register_source`/`revise_source` ("unchanged from [contracts]"), `set_publication_channel` and `register_projection_policy`. `record_terms_observation` appears nowhere in delivery-storage.md (grep-verified). Unlike the other new ports, which were fully specified there, the terms-evidence port is missing: a delivery implementer has no contract for it.
- Emission contradiction: contracts.md — "Invokes `SourceObservation.Record`; emits `ObservationRecorded`" — vs delivery-storage.md: "`ObservationRecorded`: … Emitted only by fetch-stage `record_attempt`; host receipt observations are projected from `ReceiptRecorded` alone and never emit this event." The delivery rule enumerates its emitters exhaustively (fetch worker yes, host receipts explicitly no); an operator terms observation is neither permitted nor mentioned. source-access.md's cross-check depends on the emission: "both emit existing events — `SourcePolicyChanged` and `ObservationRecorded` — consumed by `SourceAdmission` and the parsers." Two owning specs give opposite answers for the same event; an implementer cannot satisfy both.
- Payload unsatisfiable: the delivery `ObservationRecorded` payload requires "`output_id` (row-reference to `stage_outputs`)", and row-reference means "consumers must load the authoritative row for that aspect". A pre-authorization terms observation has no job and no `stage_outputs` row, so the required reference cannot be honestly populated — even a source-access-free implementation of contracts' emission would emit an event whose payload violates the delivery schema discipline.

Fix: add `record_terms_observation` to delivery-storage's Operator section with its delivery deltas, and either (a) amend the `ObservationRecorded` rule to admit terms observations with `output_id` omitted (mirroring the existing `StageOutputRecorded` compile-output precedent: "`observation_id` (omitted for compile outputs …)"), or (b) drop the emission claim from contracts and source-access's cross-check and let evidence qualification read observation rows directly — the mechanics need no event, since qualification is a revise_source-time query over `observations`.

**2. [MAJOR] Disable leaves queued-but-unleased jobs leasable, so "acquisition halt" is unenforced for that window and no spec pins the behavior.**

source-access.md: "Disabling a source is an acquisition halt, not a revocation: it stops new job enqueues for that source; in-flight leases complete and their observations are retained …" The pinned instants cover enqueue (contracts.md `enqueue_job`: "source must be enabled and allow this use"), in-flight leases (complete, retained) and gates (policies, not `enabled`). The gap is the third state: a job enqueued while enabled, still `queued` (delivery-storage job state derivation) when the disable commits. Nothing stops its lease: contracts.md `lease_job` errors are "conflict, forbidden, invalid request" with only vague "source/stage assignment authorization"; delivery-storage's `lease_job` is "unchanged from [contracts]"; the job state derivation reads no `enabled`; and the fetch execution gate reads only permission — "only while `research` is `allowed` at execution time" — not the enabled flag. So a disabled source's queued fetch can be leased and executed after disable, acquiring material under a banner the spec calls an acquisition halt. The verification list pins only "disable halting enqueues while in-flight observations are retained", not the queued case. Same defect class as round 1 finding 6 (mid-pipeline transition, unspecified behavior): either pin lease-time enabled rejection (`forbidden`) or state that queued jobs drain and acquisition truly halts at lease boundary, and add the case to verification.

**3. [MINOR] delivery-storage compile-gate phrasing is ambiguous between governing-policy and skip-to-older-allowed readings; the skip reading contradicts the revocation section.**

delivery-storage.md: "Compilation requires, for every contributing source, the current newest effective non-expired policy with `research` and `extraction` allowed; … Missing, expired, unknown or same-time conflicting policies exclude that source's inputs." "The current newest effective non-expired policy **with** research and extraction allowed" parses two ways: (a) the governing policy (greatest `effective_at` in the set), which must have both permissions allowed; (b) the newest policy among those having both allowed. Under (b), a newer `denied` policy is silently skipped in favor of an older still-non-expired `allowed` one — directly contradicting source-access.md: "Permission revocation is a later-effective registration setting the affected permission to `denied` … Its effects are exactly the delivery-storage gates: successor compiles exclude the source's inputs", and a denied policy is not "missing, expired, unknown or conflicting", so the exclusion clause would not catch it. Reading (a) is clearly the intent (source-access precedence: "The governing policy is the row with the greatest `effective_at` in that set"), but delivery-storage never links source-access for precedence — only contracts.md ("The `config_hash` recipe and access semantics are defined by [source access]") and collection-pipeline.md ("Select the newest effective non-expired policy; same-time conflicts fail closed per [source access]") do. Pre-existing wording, not introduced by this delta, but the per-instant rule now makes "current" load-bearing. One clause closes it: "the governing policy per [source access](source-access.md#precedence-and-conflict), which must have `research` and `extraction` allowed".

**4. [NOTE] Tie re-formation after an intervening later policy expires is handled by the per-instant definition but not named.**

Twins A/B at 10:00, later policy C (11:00–11:30): conflict clears at 11:00, re-forms at 11:30 when C expires and the governing set is tied again. The operative rule ("A same-time conflict exists at `T` when two or more rows in the governing set share that greatest `effective_at` …") is per-instant and decides this correctly; the clearing sentence ("clears exactly when the set stops being tied — … whichever comes first") reads as a one-time transition. Optionally add the re-tie case to the verification list for symmetry with the differing-`expires_at` case.

**5. [NOTE] Adversarial probes over the changed sections — clean, recorded so the PASS-side evidence is on file.**

- `record_terms_observation` cannot be abused to fetch arbitrary material: it performs no fetch — `body_bytes` is a caller-supplied parameter, capped at 4 MiB, `terms_url` HTTPS-only with redirects recorded and required to resolve to HTTPS, retained hash-addressed into `content_blobs` like any observation. The only pre-authorization "acquisition" is the operator's out-of-band manual inspection, a trusted human action outside the production fetcher's scope (which stays pinned to "the host of a registered source URL"). Distinct-body-hash idempotency permits unbounded distinct observation rows, but within the single trusted-operator model the Authority section already declares.
- Operator-only status is airtight against collector identities at the contracts level: the port's caller is "Operator; configuration-write authorization"; collector worker "cannot publish snapshots or change policy"; coordinator "cannot assert source facts"; publisher "cannot … alter source rights". No collector path reaches it. (The residual exposure is the delivery-surface omission in finding 1, not an authority hole.)
- Qualification is properly bound: "Qualifying evidence is an observation whose `resource_url` equals the policy's `terms_url`" — the round-1 hole (evidence from unrelated URLs) is closed, and the inaccessible-terms path pins outcome tokens (`http_error`/`source_blocked`) plus the `license_text` attestation. All three outcome tokens exist in the `observations` CHECK with compatible status pairings (`success` = 200; `http_error` = 0 or ≥400; `source_blocked` = 401/403/429/451, matching collection-pipeline's persistent-401/403 stance).
- Per-instant conflict × delivery-storage gates: consistent. The compile gate excludes "same-time conflicting" policies and re-evaluates per compile instant, so expiry-clearing ("after expiry of all but one tied row") is recognized at the later gate with no operator action; the publication gate re-evaluates "inside the publication transaction"; collection-pipeline.md:75 links the per-instant rule ("same-time conflicts fail closed per [source access](source-access.md#precedence-and-conflict)"). No contradiction.
- resolve→research mapping is defensible: `research` explicitly covers "local analysis of the retained bytes"; the resolve envelope emits `coverage_findings` rows (identity outcomes), not assertions, while parse creates assertions ("deriving typed assertions") and hence maps to `extraction`. `enqueue_job`'s "allow this use" is now pinned per stage by the source-access instants sentence, and both enqueue and lease are fail-closed with the degradation-after-lease case specified and verified.
- `not_found` addition creates no collision: it is absent from the routing-only codes ("owned exclusively by [educated routing] and no storage port raises them"); the `not_found` hits in research-artifact.md are research access-state vocabulary and the "not found" in domain-model.md's `ObservationOutcome` is the explicitly historical enumeration ("Where enumerations conflict, the delivery specs govern"), both unrelated to the error taxonomy.
- Cross-check honesty verified: "This slice adds one port (`record_terms_observation`), one error token (`not_found`, added to both closed taxonomies) and no tables, columns or events" — the no-tables claim is true (the port writes existing `observations`/`content_blobs` rows and uses existing event names); the one-port/one-token claim is accurate as of contracts.md; finding 1 is precisely that the delivery authority was not updated to match this honest tally.
