# Source access and rights

Proposed contract, source-access slice. Governs who may register source permission decisions, what evidence supports each decision, policy identity and precedence, revocation, production fetch authorization, retained audit and access-blocked re-entry. The `sources`/`source_policies` tables, the operator method surface and the `SourcePolicyChanged` event are owned by [contracts](contracts.md#callers-and-service-surface); the compile/publish gates and `snapshot_rights_audits` are owned by [delivery storage](delivery-storage.md#mutation-ports); operating limits for the fetcher are owned by [collection pipeline](collection-pipeline.md#source-rights-and-operating-limits). This spec closes the access semantics those specs reference and defines the `config_hash` recipe. Where wording conflicts, the owning spec governs.

## Authority

Permission decisions are operator actions. Only the operator port may register, revise or disable source policy; collector coordinators, workers, validators and publishers have no authority to record a permission decision, and a parser or source body can never alter one. There is exactly one trusted operator context today (the local maintainer); no operator identity model exists, so no registrar identity is recorded. Recording a registrar becomes a schema revision when a multi-operator deployment exists, not an invented field now. Registration is auditable through the immutable append-only policy row itself and the per-snapshot rights audit; nothing is mutable in place.

## Permissions

Three independent permissions per source: `research`, `extraction` and `redistribution`, each `allowed`, `denied` or `unknown`. No permission implies another and no permission defaults from another. A model or code license is evidence about the model artifact, never by itself evidence of rights over a documentation page, leaderboard, benchmark dataset or API response; data rights must be established from the terms that govern the fetched material.

- `research` authorizes acquiring material from the source into the production system: fetch jobs against the source and local analysis of the retained bytes.
- `extraction` authorizes deriving typed assertions from retained material for production use.
- `redistribution` authorizes inclusion of the source's material in `distributable` snapshots. Terms silence is not permission: absent explicit redistribution grant the value is `unknown`, and `unknown` denies distributable compilation.

`unknown` is a recorded decision meaning evidence was inspected and did not establish the permission; `denied` means evidence or operator decision forbids it. Both deny the affected production use. A permission may not be set `allowed` without retained terms evidence (below).

## Registration surface

No new policy ports. Policy decisions travel through the [contracts](contracts.md#callers-and-service-surface) operator surface, which this spec constrains:

- Initial source registration is `register_source(Source)`; `access_policy` may be absent. A source without any policy is unfetched and denied for all production uses.
- Policy registration is `revise_source(Source)` whose only changed field is `access_policy`, invoking `Source.RevisePolicy`. Validation: `terms_url` is HTTPS, `license_text` is non-empty UTF-8 of at most 65536 bytes, timestamps are UTC millisecond-aligned with `effective_at` strictly before `expires_at`, and no permission is `allowed` unless qualifying terms evidence exists (below); violation is `invalid_evidence`. The port computes `config_hash` by the recipe below and appends the immutable `source_policies` row. Idempotent by `(source_id, config_hash)`: replaying an identical policy returns the stored row's configuration hash and is not a supersession; `source_policies` rows are never revised or deleted. Emits `SourcePolicyChanged`.
- Disabling and enabling are `revise_source(Source)` with a revised enabled state, invoking `Source.Disable` or, for re-enable, `RevisePolicy` with an allowed governing policy (domain-model lifecycle; no other transitions). Replaying an identical enabled state is an idempotent no-op returning the current state, matching the publication-channel precedent. Both emit `SourcePolicyChanged`.
- Terms evidence is retained through `record_terms_observation` (contracts), the only pre-authorization acquisition path; it makes a first `allowed` registration producible.

Errors are the closed taxonomies of contracts and delivery storage: `not_found` (unknown source), `conflict` (lifecycle violation), `invalid_evidence` (evidence rule above), `invalid_request` (malformed fields). Safe-detail rules of [delivery storage](delivery-storage.md) apply; detail never includes `license_text` contents.

## Policy identity

`config_hash` is `canonical_hash` of the canonical-v1 encoding of exactly these members: `research`, `extraction`, `redistribution`, `terms_url`, `license_text`, `checked_at`, `effective_at`, `expires_at` — snake-case keys, permission tokens as strings, timestamps in the routing format `2006-01-02T15:04:05.000Z`. `source_id` is excluded (the row is keyed by it); identical policy content for different sources shares a hash by design. The hash identifies the policy content, so a re-registered identical policy is the same row and never a supersession.

## Precedence and conflict

At an evaluation instant `T` the governing set for a source is all rows with `effective_at <= T < expires_at`. The governing policy is the row with the greatest `effective_at` in that set. A same-time conflict exists at `T` when two or more rows in the governing set share that greatest `effective_at` with different `config_hash` values: no row governs and every production use of the source is denied at `T`. The conflict is per-instant and clears exactly when the set stops being tied — by registration of a strictly later-effective policy or by expiry of all but one tied row, whichever comes first; a surviving sole-maximum row then governs with no operator action. Equality by hash (identical content replay) is never a conflict. Expiry is exclusive: at `T = expires_at` the row no longer applies. An empty governing set (no policy yet, or all expired) denies every production use; absence of a policy is not unknown permission, it is no authorization.

Per-instant precedence also means a time-boxed denial's expiry re-admits an earlier still-effective allowed policy with no operator action. That is deliberate: every row is operator-authored and auditable, and the never-silently-extend rule speaks to a policy's own validity, not to the composition of overlapping operator-authored intervals. The verification list pins the case.

Evaluation instants, per stage: `enqueue_job` and `lease_job` enforce the stage's permission fail-closed at enqueue and lease acquisition — fetch and resolve stages require `research` allowed, parse stage requires `extraction` allowed, compile requires `research` and `extraction` inside the compile transaction, publication re-evaluates inside the publication transaction per [delivery storage](delivery-storage.md#mutation-ports). A permission degrading after lease acquisition does not abort the in-flight attempt — lease completion is a collection fact and its outputs are retained — but the outputs cannot reach a snapshot while the compile gate excludes the source. `checked_at` is the operator's terms-verification time, evidence only; it never governs precedence.

## Revocation

Permission revocation is a later-effective registration setting the affected permission to `denied` (or `unknown`). Its effects are exactly the delivery-storage gates: successor compiles exclude the source's inputs, the compiled-but-unpublished artifact fails the publication rights gate when the revocation committed first, an already-published artifact and its bytes remain intact and locally usable, and retained observations stay in the research record. Revocation does not delete evidence, does not rewrite `snapshot_rights_audits` of prior snapshots and does not reach into a host's adopted local generation, which is a host fact governed by `SnapshotRetention` rollback eligibility in [contracts](contracts.md#domain-events).

Disabling a source is an acquisition halt, not a revocation: it stops new job enqueues for that source and refuses new lease acquisition for its queued jobs with `forbidden` — queued work pauses until the source is enabled again — while in-flight leases complete and their observations are retained; the compile and publication gates read policies, not `enabled`, so previously retained material under a still-valid allowed policy remains compilable. Excluding a disabled source's material from snapshots requires permission revocation.

## Terms evidence

Every policy with an `allowed` permission is backed by qualifying retained evidence recorded through `record_terms_observation`. Qualifying evidence is an observation whose `resource_url` equals the policy's `terms_url`. For an `allowed` permission it is either an observation with outcome `success` capturing the terms or license bytes, or — when the terms URL is inaccessible — an observation with outcome `http_error` or `source_blocked` documenting the attempt plus the operator's attestation inside `license_text` naming what was inspected instead. Terms observations are ordinary immutable observations: body-hash-addressed bytes in the content store, never edited. A terms-page change is new evidence, not a policy change; the operator re-checks before `expires_at` and registers a successor policy. Unrechecked expiry denies production use until re-registration — the system never silently extends a policy's own validity.

## Production fetch authorization

The production fetcher issues metadata GET requests only, and only against the host of a registered source URL, only for jobs enqueued for that source, only while `research` is `allowed` at execution time. Everything else — credentials, cookies, paid APIs, POST/PUT, arbitrary user URLs, inferred endpoints — is outside the authorized scope and requires its own contract before existing. Rate limits, spacing, `Retry-After` in both formats, redirects, private-range rejection and body limits are the [collection pipeline](collection-pipeline.md#source-rights-and-operating-limits) rules; this spec adds only the authorization gate. The pre-contract research corpus (`data/` research SQLite artifact) predates this contract, is quarantined research-only and is not routing authority; nothing here retroactively authorizes it, and promotion of any of its evidence into a production snapshot requires an authorizing policy for the producing source first — the compile gate enforces this by construction.

## Coverage and re-entry

A source whose fetch cannot run because authorization denies it carries `coverage_findings` rows with outcome `blocked` and a detail naming the denied permission; blocked findings name retry conditions (`policy_registered`, `source_enabled`) rather than a wall-clock guess. `SourcePolicyChanged` drives `SourceAdmission` to enqueue refresh work for the source when a governing policy appears, and to leave coverage explicitly blocked otherwise. The event payload carries no time fields and no conflict information, so `SourceAdmission` re-evaluates precedence over the `source_policies` rows at consumption time; the event is a trigger, never the decision. A blocked source is never silently dropped from coverage reports: the inventory keeps the channel row with its outcome.

## Verification before acceptance

Replay these against real Postgres with the contracts DDL: the bootstrap path — register source, `record_terms_observation` on the terms URL, then first `allowed` policy accepted; `allowed` without terms evidence rejected `invalid_evidence`; evidence observation whose `resource_url` differs from `terms_url` rejected; terms-inaccessible registration with `http_error`/`source_blocked` observation and attestation accepted; idempotent policy replay returning the stored configuration hash; hash-recipe fixtures generated under the independent JCS rule; same-time conflict denying all uses at `T`, clearing after a later-effective registration, after expiry of all but one tied row with differing `expires_at`, and re-forming when an intervening later policy expires and the set ties again; expiry boundary `T = expires_at - 1ms` allowed and `T = expires_at` denied; time-boxed denial expiring and re-admitting the earlier still-effective allowed policy; parse enqueue denied when `extraction` is denied and fetch enqueue denied when `research` is denied, both fail-closed at lease too; permission degradation after lease acquisition completing the attempt with outputs retained but excluded from compile; revocation before publication failing the rights gate and after publication leaving the artifact intact; compile exclusion of expired, unknown, missing and conflicting sources with the remaining sources' `snapshot_rights_audits` rows pinning the exact authorizing hash; disable halting enqueues, refusing lease acquisition for queued jobs, while in-flight observations are retained and previously retained material remaining compilable under a valid policy; and the coverage report showing blocked channels with retry conditions rather than gaps.

## Cross-check

Transitions trace through all three contracts: policy registration and lifecycle revision ride the existing `register_source`/`revise_source` operator ports; terms evidence rides the new `record_terms_observation` operator port (contracts); both ride existing surfaces — `SourcePolicyChanged` consumed by `SourceAdmission`, and the `observations` rows read directly by policy-evidence qualification; terms observations emit no event, because delivery-storage reserves `ObservationRecorded` for fetch-stage workers. Compile and publication consume precedence decisions through the delivery-storage gates and `snapshot_rights_audits`. This slice adds one port (`record_terms_observation`), one error token (`not_found`, added to both closed taxonomies) and no tables, columns or events. The `config_hash` recipe is the single definition; contracts and delivery storage reference it.
